Lesson 0006 · Phase 2: Core patterns · ~10 minutes

Chain-of-thought

Intermediate steps as test-time compute. What the paper actually claimed.

Your one win today State Wei et al.’s claim without folklore: CoT is few-shot exemplars of intermediate reasoning, it helps sufficiently large models, and it is the substrate under every agent that “thinks out loud.”

1 · What the paper is

Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models (Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le, Zhou — NeurIPS 2022).[1]

Chain of thought A series of intermediate natural-language reasoning steps that lead to a final answer. CoT prompting = few-shot exemplars of the form ⟨input, chain of thought, output⟩, not a new architecture.

The empirical headline: PaLM 540B with eight CoT exemplars hit then-SOTA on GSM8K math word problems, beating even a finetuned GPT-3 that used a verifier.[1] Gains showed up on arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic tasks.

Why it works, in the paper’s own framing:

  1. Multi-step problems can be decomposed; extra tokens of “thinking” are extra test-time compute allocated to hard items.
  2. The chain is an interpretable window (you can see where it went wrong — you still cannot see the actual activations).
  3. It applies, in principle, to any task humans solve in language.
  4. It is elicited from off-the-shelf large models by exemplars. No finetune required.

2 · What the paper is not

Three pieces of folklore to kill now, because interviews punish them.

  • Not “let’s think step by step.” That zero-shot trigger is Kojima et al. 2022, a different paper. Wei et al. used few-shot worked examples.
  • Not free, not universal. The original results described CoT as an emergent ability of scale: small models produced fluent but illogical chains, and CoT could hurt them. (Frontier models have since shifted the threshold; the caution remains: do not cargo-cult CoT onto a tiny local model and expect GSM8K magic.)[1]
  • Not grounded. CoT reasons inside the model’s weights. If a fact is missing or stale, the chain will still sound sure. That failure is why the next lesson exists.

3 · Why agents care

Every later pattern spends tokens on intermediate text on purpose. ReAct (0007) is CoT interleaved with actions. Reflexion (0008) is CoT about a failed trajectory. “Reasoning models” in 2025–26 are CoT trained to run longer before answering.[2] You are not learning a trick. You are learning the substrate.

Interview angle “Why have the model think out loud?” Strong: CoT allocates test-time compute to intermediate steps; it is also the only human-readable trace of a decision. Weak: “it makes the model smarter.” Name Wei et al., name the scale caveat, name the grounding limit that ReAct fixes.

4 · Test-time compute, after the paper

Wei et al. spent extra tokens by pasting worked examples. Since 2025 the same idea is often a thinking budget: the model is trained to emit a long internal trace before the answer, and the product meters that trace as test-time compute.[3] Three distinctions interviewers now check:

  • Few-shot CoT (this paper) is exemplars in the prompt. You can read every step. No extra training.
  • A reasoning model’s trace may be hidden, summarized, or billed separately. It is still tokens sampled before the answer. It is not a few-shot exemplar you wrote.
  • Neither is grounded. A longer trace does not fetch a missing fact. That limit is unchanged, and it is why lesson 0007 exists.

Say the cost out loud: you are buying accuracy on hard items by paying latency and tokens on every item that thinks. Do not turn a thinking budget on for a lookup the tool should have done.

5 · Retrieval practice

1. In Wei et al., chain-of-thought prompting means…

Correct. ⟨input, chain, output⟩ exemplars in the prompt. No new weights, no special decoder.
The paper's method is prompting with worked examples, not finetuning or a numeric decoder. Option 2.

2. A real limit of CoT, as later used against it by ReAct?

Correct. CoT is a static black box over internal knowledge. That is the gap ReAct fills with actions.
CoT is English (or any language), and the original claim was it helps *large* models. The limit is grounding. Option 3.

3. “Let’s think step by step” with no exemplars is…

Correct. Cite the right paper. Wei = few-shot worked examples. Kojima = zero-shot trigger. Both exist; they are not the same claim.
Wei et al. used few-shot exemplars. The zero-shot trigger is a later, different result. Option 1.

4. A 2025 “reasoning model” thinking trace differs from Wei et al. how?

Correct. Same family (test-time compute), different mechanism. Still ungrounded. Tools remain lesson 0007.
Not retrieval, and not Wei’s few-shot paste. A trained or metered trace. Option 3.

6 · Read next (primary source)

Wei et al. — CoT paper, PDF. Read the abstract, §1, and §2 (about 12 minutes). Skip the tables on a first pass; you need the claim, not GSM8K decimals. Then Weng — Why We Think (1 May 2025) for what “thinking” became after the paper.

7 · Lock it in

  • Write, from memory: “CoT = few-shot intermediate steps; helps large models; ungrounded.”
  • New glossary terms: chain of thought, test-time compute, exemplar.

On paper: one question where a chain of thought invents a date, then the same question with a tool observation that corrects it. That is the gap before ReAct.
