Lesson 0015 · Phase 4: Advanced · ~12 minutes

Multi-agent systems

Orchestrator–worker. A 90% win that is mostly extra tokens. Know when that is a bad deal.

Your one win today State Anthropic’s Research result honestly: multi-agent beat single-agent by 90.2% on their internal eval, token use explained ~80% of BrowseComp variance, and multi-agent costs ~15× a chat. Then say when not to pay.

1 · What they actually built

Anthropic, How we built our multi-agent research system. A multi-agent system, in their wording, is multiple agents — LLMs using tools in a loop — working together.[1]

Research is an orchestrator–worker (lesson 0003) grown into true agents: a lead plans, then spawns parallel subagents. Each subagent has its own window, tools, and prompt, explores one direction, and compresses findings back. The lead synthesizes.

Why subagents help search “The essence of search is compression.” Parallel workers buy you extra context capacity and reduce path dependence: one bad branch does not poison every other branch’s window.

On their internal research eval, Opus 4 lead + Sonnet 4 subagents beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2%. On BrowseComp, three factors explained 95% of performance variance; token usage alone explained 80%, then tool-call count, then model choice.[1] Read that twice. “Collaboration” is mostly “we spent more tokens, in parallel, in isolated windows.”

Cost they published: agents ≈ 4× chat tokens; multi-agent ≈ 15× chat. The latest, more efficient models multiply whatever budget you already spend — upgrading the model beat doubling the budget on an older one, in their telling.

2 · When this is a bad deal

  • Shared, mutating state. Coding edits that collide. A ledger. Anything where two workers can stomp each other. Anthropic flags tight sequential dependencies as a poor fit.
  • Narrow, enumerable tasks. If a workflow in lesson 0003 covers it, parallel agents are a raise you did not need to give.
  • You cannot afford 15×. Breadth-first research is the sweet spot they name. Depth-first, single-path jobs want one good agent (or a chain).
  • You have not eval’d the single-agent baseline. 90.2% is versus a baseline they measured. “Let’s add agents” without that number is theater.

Willison’s subagent notes, from the coding-agent side, rhyme: isolate a job (Explore), run specialists in parallel when the branches do not share writes, keep the parent as the compressor.[2]

A planner plus a generator plus an evaluator (0017) is a different split. Those roles run in sequence so the grader does not share the builder’s context. They are not the 15× fan-out in this lesson. Do not cite the research-system number for that harness, or the other way around.

Interview angle “Should we go multi-agent?” Strong: only for breadth-first work that benefits from isolated windows; cite 90.2% and the 80% token story so you don’t mystify it; cite 15× cost; refuse it for colliding writes; demand a single-agent eval first. Weak: “multi-agent is more intelligent.”

3 · Retrieval practice

1. Anthropic’s 90.2% gain is best explained as…

Correct. Token usage explained 80% of BrowseComp variance. Compression across extra windows is the mechanism.
Not democracy, not social IQ. Extra tokens in isolated contexts. Option 3.

2. Multi-agent is a poor fit when…

Correct. Shared writes and tight sequences are the documented anti-fit. Breadth-first is the fit; a weak baseline is a reason to try, not to refuse.
Breadth-first is the sweet spot, and a measured weak baseline is why you'd consider it. Colliding state is the no. Option 1.

3. Relative token cost they published?

Correct. 4× and 15×. The 90.2% is a quality delta on one eval, not a price multiplier.
Do not invent a cache discount, and do not turn 90.2% into a bill. Option 2.

4. Orchestrator–workers (workflow) vs this Research system?

Correct. Same topology, deeper nodes: each worker is tools-in-a-loop, not a single LLM call. That is why the bill jumps.
Not identical to the simple workflow, and not a chain. Workers are agents. Option 3.

5 · Lock it in

  • 90.2%, 80% tokens, 15× cost, poor fit = colliding writes. Recite.
  • New glossary terms: subagent, orchestrator–worker (multi-agent), context isolation.
