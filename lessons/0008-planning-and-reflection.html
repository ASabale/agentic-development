Lesson 0008 · Phase 2: Core patterns · ~12 minutes

Planning and reflection

Decompose the job. Then critique the trajectory — in language, not in weights.

Your one win today Separate three verbs interviewers mash together: decompose (plan the work), reason (ReAct thoughts), reflect (verbal feedback stored for the next trial). Cite Reflexion for the third.

1 · Planning is decomposition

Lilian Weng’s durable taxonomy of an LLM agent has three components: planning, memory, tool use.[1] Planning itself splits in two:

Subgoal and decomposition

Break a large task into smaller ones the loop can actually finish. CoT is the cheap version (“think step by step”). Tree-of-Thoughts explores several next thoughts. You can also just prompt: “List the subgoals.”

Reflection and refinement

Look at what already happened, criticize it, and change the next attempt. This is not the same as a ReAct thought, which is in-trial working memory. Reflection is across trials.

Production note, which 12-factor Factor 10 will repeat: a plan with 100 steps is a context problem, not a bravery problem. Prefer a small agent that finishes a subgoal over a monologue that plans the quarter.[2]

Anthropic’s orchestrator–workers (lesson 0003) is planning-as-a-workflow: the lead model decomposes this input, workers execute, the lead synthesizes. You do not need a planning paper to ship that.

2 · Reflexion: verbal reinforcement learning

Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning (Shinn, Cassano, Berman, Gopinath, Narasimhan, Yao — NeurIPS 2023).[3]

Reflexion After a trial, convert the feedback signal (pass/fail, a unit test, a score) into a verbal self-critique. Store that text in an episodic memory buffer. On the next trial, the actor conditions on those critiques. No weight updates.

The paper’s slogan: reinforce language agents through linguistic feedback. A failed HumanEval run plus the failing tests becomes a paragraph — “I mutated the input in place; next time copy first” — that the next attempt can read. Claimed result: 91% pass@1 on HumanEval, vs a GPT-4 baseline at 80%.[3]

Two caveats you must carry into an interview:

  • The headline number leans on external feedback (unit tests). Self-critique without a ground signal can congratulate a wrong answer. LLMs are mediocre at catching their own mistakes; ablations in the paper explore this.
  • Reflections occupy the window. Keep three, not thirty. That is the same compaction instinct as lesson 0004.

3 · Where this sits in the loop

VerbWhenWhere it lives
DecomposeBefore or as you startA plan in context, or an orchestrator’s subtask list
Reason (ReAct thought)Inside a trial, between actionsThe current transcript
Reflect (Reflexion)After a trial fails or endsAn episodic buffer, re-injected next trial

Native planning is enough when the job fits one session. On multi-hour work, Anthropic’s 2026 harness keeps a separate planner that writes the spec, and a separate evaluator that does not grade its own output.[3] That is not “more CoT.” It is another context, paid for because the builder’s plan drifts. Lesson 0017 is the full split. Use it when the horizon is the problem, not as a default on a ten-step ticket.

Interview angle “How would your agent get better over retries?” Weak: “I’d finetune.” Strong: if you have a checkable signal (tests, a grader), write a short verbal critique into memory and retry — Reflexion. If you do not have a signal, do not trust the model to mark its own homework. And cap retries (Factor 9). On a multi-hour job, say the planner is a different agent, not a longer prompt.

4 · Retrieval practice

1. Reflexion improves the next trial by…

Correct. Verbal RL: linguistic feedback in an episodic buffer. Weights do not move.
No training step. The “gradient” is a paragraph. Option 3.

2. A ReAct thought and a Reflexion critique differ because…

Correct. Same language channel, different timescale. Do not mash the verbs.
Neither updates weights, and neither requires a model count. Timescale is the split. Option 1.

3. Why is “just have it reflect” dangerous without a test?

Correct. Reflexion’s headline used unit tests as the reward. Self-grading is a weak signal.
Billing and frameworks are irrelevant. The risk is ungrounded self-congratulation. Option 2.

4. Best production default for a 40-step plan?

Correct. Factor 10: as context grows, models get lost. Decomposition is also a context strategy.
A giant plan in one window is how agents wander. Splitting is the plan. Option 3.

5 · Read next (primary source)

Shinn et al. — Reflexion (abstract + introduction). For the taxonomy this lesson sits in: Weng, LLM Powered Autonomous Agents, the Planning section only.

6 · Lock it in

  • Recite the three verbs and their timescales.
  • New glossary terms: decomposition, Reflexion, episodic memory, verbal RL.

Want a tiny example — fail a function, write a critique, retry? Ask. Keep it on paper; we are still theory-first.
